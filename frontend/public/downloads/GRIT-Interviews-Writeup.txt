GRIT Interviews: Write-up
=========================

Aryan Jain | NIAT AI Product Engineer, Round 1 | Live app: http://130.210.56.15 | Code:
github.com/aryanjain1908/Interview-Scheduler

A student who clears every skill for a Track Badge has to pass a live interview to earn it.
I built the booking and interview flow for that. Students book a slot, interviewers take
the interview and record the result, and the GRIT team can see who's stuck and take a badge
back if cheating comes out later. This write-up covers the states, the edge cases I handled
and the data model.


1. States
---------

Each student has one journey per badge. Only one module (the state machine) is allowed to
change a journey's state, and every change is saved with who made it and why. The same log
is shown to the student as their timeline.

* Not eligible
    What it means: Some skills for the badge aren't cleared yet
    What moves it on: Last skill cleared → Eligible
* Eligible
    What it means: Can book, nothing booked right now
    What moves it on: Student books a slot → Booked
* Booked
    What it means: Has a slot with an interviewer
    What moves it on: Interviewer starts it → In progress. Student or interviewer cancels →
    Eligible. Student marked absent → No-show. Interviewer never joins, or nobody turns up
    → Eligible (no attempt used, first in line to rebook). Reschedules keep it Booked
* In progress
    What it means: Interview is happening
    What moves it on: Result recorded → Passed, Not cleared or Retake later. No result
    within 24 hours → Eligible (closed as missed, no attempt used)
* Passed
    What it means: Badge is live on the profile
    What moves it on: Malpractice confirmed → Revoked
* Retake later
    What it means: "Not ready yet". Doesn't use an attempt
    What moves it on: The date the interviewer picked (1 to 60 days) → Eligible
* Not cleared
    What it means: Uses 1 of 3 attempts
    What moves it on: 14 days later → Eligible, if attempts are left
* No-show
    What it means: Student didn't join. Uses an attempt
    What moves it on: 3 days later → Eligible. If the team accepts the student's dispute →
    Eligible at once, attempt given back
* Revoked
    What it means: Badge taken back for malpractice
    What moves it on: Appeal accepted → Passed

Interview day. Students can't book a slot that starts in the next 30 minutes, unless the
interviewer opens it at short notice (down to 5 minutes). The interviewer can start 10
minutes early and record a result once the slot begins. 15 minutes after the start, the
interviewer can mark a no-show and the student can report that the interviewer never came.
If nobody has started it 15 minutes after the slot ends, it's closed as missed and no
attempt is used.


2. Edge cases and questions
---------------------------

BOOKING
* Two students click the same slot at the same moment. The row is locked while booking, so
  one gets it and the other is told the slot was just taken. A unique index on live bookings
  is a backup check in case the code has a bug.
* One live booking per badge. A student can still hold bookings for different badges at the
  same time.
* Slots are offered 14 days ahead. The 30-minute notice above applies to every slot unless
  the interviewer chooses to open it sooner.

CHANGING A BOOKING
* Students can reschedule twice and cancel freely until 12 hours before. Inside 12 hours
  they get one emergency change per attempt, with a reason. The change is applied right away
  and the team reviews it later. If approved it's free, if rejected it counts as an attempt.
* Moving to the same time with a different interviewer is refused. It would waste a change
  without fixing anything.
* An interviewer can move or cancel an interview but has to give a reason. The student is
  told why, keeps their attempt, and goes to the front of the queue. If the student had an
  emergency change waiting for review, it's closed as approved, since it wasn't the student's
  fault.
* If the student and the interviewer change the same interview at the same moment, only the
  first change goes through. The second person is asked to refresh.

ON THE DAY
* A no-show can only be marked 15 minutes into the slot, needs a short note, and can't be
  marked once the interview has started. The meeting link gives me no attendance data, so the
  student can dispute a no-show within 48 hours and the team decides after hearing both
  sides.
* Reminders go out a day before and an hour before, and they say the real time left, so a
  short-notice booking made 15 minutes ahead doesn't claim it's an hour away.

AFTER THE INTERVIEW
* Only the assigned interviewer can record the result, once. A "Not cleared" or "Retake
  later" result needs written feedback. Internal notes are never shown to the student.
* If cheating is found after a pass, the team revokes the badge and the student sees the
  reason. If their appeal is accepted, the badge comes back.

ASSUMPTIONS AND OPEN QUESTIONS
* Skill clears come from the existing contest system. The team can correct them by hand,
  and every correction is logged.
* 30-minute slots, 3 attempts per badge and the 14-day and 3-day waits are just config
  values for now. They aren't final product decisions.
* If a skill clear is withdrawn while a student is booked, I keep the booking. That
  probably needs a product call.
* For the demo anyone can sign up with any role. In production the GRIT team would assign
  roles.
* Meetings use a generated Jitsi link and messages go to the in-app inbox. Email or
  WhatsApp would plug into the same notify step.
* All times are stored in UTC and shown in IST.


3. Data model
-------------

Postgres, with Alembic migrations. Status columns are plain text checked in code, so adding
a status doesn't need a database enum change.

* users
    Key fields: email (unique), password_hash, role
    Why it exists: Students, interviewers and the GRIT team
* badges, skills, badge_skills
    Key fields: badge and skill ids, max_attempts
    Why it exists: A skill can count towards more than one badge
* student_skill_clears
    Key fields: student_id, skill_id, cleared_at
    Why it exists: Decides who is eligible
* interview_journeys
    Key fields: student_id + badge_id (unique), status, attempts_used, reschedules_used,
    eligible_again_at, priority_rebook
    Why it exists: The state machine's row, one per student per badge
* interview_events
    Key fields: journey_id, from_status, to_status, actor_id, reason
    Why it exists: Audit trail and the student's timeline
* interviewer_badges
    Key fields: interviewer_id, badge_id
    Why it exists: Which badges an interviewer can take
* availability_windows
    Key fields: interviewer_id, weekday or date, start and end time
    Why it exists: The hours an interviewer is free
* slots
    Key fields: interviewer_id + start_at (unique), status
    Why it exists: 30-minute blocks: open, booked or blocked
* interviews
    Key fields: journey_id, slot_id, attempt_no, status, meeting_link, no-show and dispute
    fields
    Why it exists: One row per booking. Unique indexes stop double booking
* change_requests
    Key fields: interview_id, reason, from and to time, status
    Why it exists: Emergency changes waiting for the team
* interview_outcomes
    Key fields: interview_id, result, scores, feedback, internal_notes
    Why it exists: One result per interview
* student_badges, malpractice_flags
    Key fields: status, reason, resolution
    Why it exists: What the profile shows, and why a badge was taken back
* notifications
    Key fields: user_id, kind, title, body, read_at
    Why it exists: The in-app inbox

How they connect: a user has one journey per badge, a journey can have several interviews
over time (retakes and rebookings), each interview takes one slot and gets at most one
outcome, and every status change adds a row to interview_events.
